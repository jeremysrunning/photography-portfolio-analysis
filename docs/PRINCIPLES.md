# Principles

These principles guide both the software and the research behind it.

---

## Analyze Portfolios, Not Photographs

Individual photographs are anecdotes.

Portfolios reveal patterns.

This project focuses on discovering habits and tendencies across a body of work rather than evaluating individual images.

---

## Measure, Don't Judge

This project does not score photographs.

Instead, it measures characteristics that help photographers better understand their own work.

The goal is insight, not evaluation.

---

## Separate Evidence From Interpretation

Measurements should be reproducible.

Observations should be clearly identified as interpretations.

Both are valuable, but they are not the same.

---

## Source Agnostic

The origin of a portfolio should not affect the analysis.

Whether photographs come from a website, Lightroom, a local folder, or another source, the analysis should produce consistent results.

---

## Metadata Over Media

Whenever possible, retain metadata and derived measurements instead of storing original images.

Temporary image access for analysis is acceptable.

Permanent image archiving is outside the scope of this project.

---

## Curiosity Over Confirmation

The most valuable discoveries are the unexpected ones.

The project should help photographers uncover habits they didn't know they had rather than simply confirming existing beliefs.

---

## Neutral Language

Reports should describe.

Never prescribe.

For example:

✓ "Centered compositions became more common."

Not:

✗ "Composition quality improved."

---

## Confidence Matters

Every finding should communicate confidence.

Evidence should be proportional to the strength of the conclusion.

---

## Build for Discovery

Every feature should answer a meaningful question about a photographer's work.

If a feature doesn't help someone understand their photography more deeply, it probably doesn't belong.
